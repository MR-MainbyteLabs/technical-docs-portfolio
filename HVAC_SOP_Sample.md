## SOP Sample Set — Summit Comfort Heating & Air

Fictional company built as a portfolio sample for the SOP & Operations Documentation service ($97–$175). Three documents: customer call script, technician daily report template, and complaint handling procedure.

## Document 1: Customer Call Script — Incoming Service Requests {#doc-1-customer-call-script}

**Purpose:** Standardize how dispatch handles incoming calls so every customer gets a consistent, professional experience and dispatch captures everything a technician needs before arriving on\-site.

**Owner:** Dispatch / Customer Service
**Applies to:** All incoming calls for service requests, quotes, and emergencies

### Step 1 — Greeting {#step-1-greeting}

"Thank you for calling Summit Comfort Heating and Air, this is \[Name\], how can I help you today?"

### Step 2 — Identify call type {#step-2-identify-call-type}

Ask: "Is this for a repair, a maintenance visit, or a new installation quote?"

Route based on answer:

| Call type | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| Repair (system not working) | Go to Step 3 — Emergency Screening |
| Maintenance / tune\-up | Go to Step 4 — Standard Scheduling |
| New install / quote | Transfer to Sales, or take contact info if Sales unavailable |

### Step 3 — Emergency screening (repairs only) {#step-3-emergency-screening-repairs-only}

Ask these three questions, in order:

1. "Is there any smell of gas, or smoke?" — If yes, instruct the customer to leave the house immediately and call the gas company/911. Do not schedule a visit. Log as EMERGENCY — GAS.
2. "Is anyone in the home elderly, an infant, or medically vulnerable to extreme temperature?" — If yes, flag as PRIORITY.
3. "What is the current indoor temperature, if you know it?" — Record for the technician.

### Step 4 — Standard scheduling {#step-4-standard-scheduling}

Collect, in order:

1. Full name and service address
2. Best callback number
3. Description of the issue in the customer's own words (write it verbatim — do not paraphrase)
4. Preferred appointment window (offer two options, do not ask "when works for you" open\-ended)
5. Confirm: "You're all set for \[day/window\]. A technician will call 20–30 minutes before arrival. Is there anything else I can help with?"

### Step 5 — Close {#step-5-close}

"Thank you for choosing Summit Comfort — you'll get a text confirmation shortly."

**Never say:** "I don't know" without following up with "let me find out and call you back within the hour." Never give a repair cost estimate over the phone — that's the technician's call after diagnosis.

## Document 2:Technician Daily Report {#doc-2-technician-daily-report}

**Purpose:** Capture a consistent record of every job for billing accuracy, warranty documentation, and quality tracking.

**Owner:** Field Technicians
**Submitted:** End of each work day, before clocking out

### Fields to complete per job {#fields-to-complete-per-job}

| Field | Instructions |
| --- | --- |
| Job ID / Customer name | From dispatch ticket |
| Arrival time / Departure time | Actual times, not scheduled window |
| System type | e.g. split system, heat pump, furnace, mini\-split |
| Diagnosis | What was actually wrong — be specific (e.g. "capacitor failed" not "AC not cooling") |
| Work performed | List every action taken, in order |
| Parts used | Part name, quantity, and part number if available |
| Parts needed but not on truck | Flag for follow\-up visit |
| Customer signature | Required before leaving, confirms work performed and satisfaction |
| Photos | Before/after photo of any replaced part or major repair — required for jobs over $200 |
| Follow\-up required | Yes/No — if yes, note what and when |

### End\-of\-day checklist {#end-of-day-checklist}

- [ ] All job reports submitted for today's completed jobs
- [ ] Truck inventory updated for any parts used
- [ ] Any incomplete jobs flagged to dispatch with reason
- [ ] Vehicle fuel and mileage logged

**Why this matters:** incomplete reports are the \#1 cause of billing disputes and warranty claim denials. A vague diagnosis ("fixed AC") gives the office nothing to invoice against and nothing to defend if the customer disputes the charge.

## Document 3:Complaint Handling Procedure {#doc-3-complaint-handling-procedure}

**Purpose:** Resolve customer complaints quickly and consistently, protect the company's reputation, and prevent small issues from becoming reviews or chargebacks.

**Owner:** Office Manager (escalation point), all staff (first response)

### Step 1 — Acknowledge within 1 business hour {#step-1-acknowledge-within-1-business-hour}

Whoever receives the complaint (phone, email, review, text) responds within 1 hour during business hours, even if just to say: "I've received this and I'm looking into it — I'll follow up by \[specific time\] today."

### Step 2 — Classify severity {#step-2-classify-severity}

| Severity | Definition | Response owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1 | Scheduling issue, minor miscommunication | Dispatch resolves directly |
| Level 2 | Work quality concern, return visit needed | Office Manager \+ original technician |
| Level 3 | Safety concern, major billing dispute, threat of legal/review action | Owner notified immediately |

### Step 3 — Investigate before responding {#step-3-investigate-before-responding}

Pull the technician daily report for the job in question. Do not respond to the customer with a resolution until you've reviewed what was actually documented — assumptions here are how complaints get worse.

### Step 4 — Resolve {#step-4-resolve}

Standard resolution options, in order of preference:

1. Free return visit to correct the issue (no charge if within 30 days of original service)
2. Partial refund if the issue was a documented technician error
3. Full refund only for Level 3 issues or repeat failures on the same job

Never offer a refund before confirming what happened. Never argue with a customer about whether they're right — resolve first, review internally after.

### Step 5 — Close the loop {#step-5-close-the-loop}

Once resolved, call (don't just email) the customer to confirm satisfaction. Log the resolution in the customer file. If the complaint was Level 2 or 3, the Office Manager reviews it with the technician involved within the same week — not as discipline, as a pattern check.

### Step 6 — Prevent recurrence {#step-6-prevent-recurrence}

Any complaint type that occurs 3\+ times in a month gets flagged to the Owner as a process issue, not a one\-off technician issue.
