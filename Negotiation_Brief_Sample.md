# Negotiation Brief: Severance Package Negotiation

**Prepared for:** Renee Castillo
**Situation:** Laid off after 6 years; severance offer received with a signing deadline
**Date:** September 8, 2026
**Signing deadline on offer:** 21 calendar days from receipt (standard consideration period)

## The Situation This Brief Responds To

Renee was laid off Tuesday after six years, along with four others in her department, in a round the company described as a "restructuring." She was handed a severance agreement the same day: four weeks of pay, no COBRA help, and a release of all legal claims against the company, with 21 days to sign. Her instinct — like most people's in this moment — was to sign quickly and move on. She's worried that asking for more will look ungrateful, or that the offer is final because it was printed on letterhead.

It isn't final. Severance offers are almost always a first draft, not a policy. The company wrote a release that protects itself from lawsuits — asking for more in exchange for signing it isn't confrontational, it's the actual negotiation the document exists to have. The risk isn't asking; it's signing in week one out of fear, when the only real deadline is week three.

## Your Leverage

- The company announced record quarterly profits the same week as the layoffs — this was a cost decision, not a performance one, and Renee's file backs that up.
- Renee's last three performance reviews all read "exceeds expectations," with no documented issues — nothing in her record justifies a minimal package.
- She has 6 years of tenure; many severance formulas scale with tenure, and four weeks is below typical scaling (roughly one to two weeks per year of service) for this length of employment.
- The 21\-day consideration period is a floor set by federal age\-discrimination law for individual releases, not a rush deadline — using the full window costs nothing and signals she's taking it seriously, not stalling.

## The Numbers

Decide these before responding. Share only the opening ask.

|  | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walk\-away floor | 8 weeks pay \+ 3 months COBRA premium covered | Below this, not worth signing away legal claims for |
| Target (realistic goal) | 10 weeks pay \+ 6 months COBRA covered | Reflects tenure\-scaled severance norms plus a bridge to next coverage |
| Opening ask | 12 weeks pay \+ 6 months COBRA \+ neutral reference letter | Leaves room to concede while protecting the two priorities: income runway and COBRA gap |

Non\-pay levers if the company won't move on weeks of pay: a neutral reference agreement (company confirms only dates and title if contacted), payout of unused PTO if not already included, or outplacement/career\-transition support.

## Talking Points, By Phase

**Opening the response** (email first, to create a written record)
"Thank you for putting this together. I'd like to take some time to review it carefully, and I have a few questions about the severance terms before I sign anything."

**Making the ask**
"Given my six years here and the fact that this was a cost\-driven restructuring rather than a performance issue, I'd like to request 12 weeks of severance pay along with 6 months of COBRA premium coverage, and a neutral reference agreement going forward."

**If HR says the package is standard/non\-negotiable**
"I understand there's a standard formula. I'd still like this considered given my tenure and review history — is there someone with authority to revisit the terms, or a process for that?"

**If they offer a partial increase (e.g., 6 weeks) and hold there**
"I appreciate that movement. COBRA coverage matters as much to me as the pay difference — would extending that to 4–6 months be possible even if the pay stays at 6 weeks?"

**If they say the offer expires before the 21\-day window is up**
"My understanding is the consideration period is 21 days under the release terms — I want to make sure I'm using that time to review properly, including with an attorney, before responding further." (This is true and worth stating plainly — a company pushing an earlier deadline than its own release states is a flag, not a reason to rush.)

**Closing, once terms are agreed**
"That works — please send the finalized agreement reflecting the updated terms in writing before I sign." Never sign based on a verbal promise of revised terms.

## Objection Responses

| If they say... | You say... |
| --- | --- |
| "This package is standard for everyone in the restructuring." | "I understand the formula — I'd still like my tenure and performance history considered as part of this." |
| "We don't have budget to increase severance." | "I understand budget is a factor. Is there flexibility on COBRA coverage or the reference terms instead of the pay amount?" |
| "The offer is only valid for 48 hours." | "The release states a 21\-day consideration period — I intend to use that time to review the terms carefully, including with an attorney." |
| "Everyone else already signed at this rate." | "I can only speak to my own situation and tenure — I'm not asking based on what others agreed to." |

## What Not to Say

- Don't sign on the day you receive the offer "to get it over with" — the consideration period exists specifically so you don't have to decide under pressure.
- Don't frame the ask emotionally ("this isn't fair after everything I did") — frame it on tenure, performance record, and market norms; it's harder to dismiss.
- Don't threaten legal action unless you've actually consulted an employment attorney and mean it — an empty threat undermines the rest of the conversation.
- Don't discuss what a specific coworker received — negotiate your own tenure and record, not a comparison that can be denied or disputed.

## Next Steps

1. Do not sign anything in the first few days — use the written consideration period in full; it costs nothing and the company already agreed to it by including it.
2. Send the email above within the first week to create a paper trail and signal you're engaging seriously, not stalling indefinitely.
3. Before signing the final release — regardless of what negotiation achieves — have an employment attorney review it. A brief like this covers negotiation strategy, not the legal effect of the waiver itself.
4. Get any revised terms in writing from HR before signing, and confirm the final signed date starts any post\-signing revocation period correctly.
