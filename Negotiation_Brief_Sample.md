# Severance Negotiation Brief
**Prepared for:** Daniel Reyes
**Position:** Senior Operations Coordinator
**Employer:** Caldwell Logistics Group
**Date of Notice:** March 18, 2024
**Response Deadline:** April 1, 2024

---

## Situation Summary

Daniel Reyes was notified of position elimination on March 18, 2024 after 
six years with Caldwell Logistics Group. The initial severance offer is two 
weeks of base pay ($3,307.69) and continuation of health benefits through 
March 31 — four days from the date of notice.

This brief outlines the basis for a counteroffer, the target terms, and the 
recommended approach for the negotiation conversation.

---

## Basis for Counteroffer

**Tenure:** Six years of continuous employment is a meaningful anchor. 
Industry standard for negotiated severance in non-executive roles is 
one to two weeks per year of service. On that basis, six to twelve weeks 
is a defensible range.

**Role contribution:** Daniel managed carrier relationships across three 
regional lanes and cross-trained two junior coordinators in the past 18 
months. Operational continuity during transition has direct dollar value 
to the employer.

**Benefits gap:** A benefits cutoff of March 31 — four days after notice — 
leaves no realistic window to secure replacement coverage. COBRA continuation 
is available but costs approximately $680/month without employer contribution. 
This is a legitimate and sympathetic point to raise.

**Timing:** The offer was made mid-quarter. Daniel has no pending PTO payout 
noted in the offer letter. Confirm whether accrued PTO is owed under 
Pennsylvania wage payment law — if so, that is not negotiable, it is owed.

---

## Target Terms

| Item | Initial Offer | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Severance pay | 2 weeks ($3,307.69) | 8 weeks ($13,230.76) |
| Benefits continuation | Through March 31 | Through June 30 (90 days) |
| Outplacement support | Not offered | Career transition assistance or $500 stipend |
| Reference letter | Not offered | Written letter, agreed language |
| Non-disparagement | Mutual (standard) | Confirm mutual — not one-sided |

---

## Recommended Approach

**Open with appreciation, not grievance.** The goal is a better outcome, 
not a confrontation. Daniel's six years and clean record are assets in 
this conversation — lead with them.

**Anchor on tenure first.** "I've given six years to this team and I want 
to transition well. I'd like to talk about whether we can get closer to 
the standard range for someone at my tenure level."

**Raise benefits second, separately.** "The benefits cutoff is the part 
that puts me in the most difficult position practically. Four days doesn't 
give me time to make a plan. Is there flexibility to extend that through 
the end of June?"

**Do not accept or sign anything at the first meeting.** Ask for 48 hours 
to review any revised offer. This is normal and expected.

**If they say the offer is final:** "I understand. Can I have that in 
writing so I can review it with someone before I sign?" This is always 
a reasonable request.

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not lead with legal threats — it closes doors before negotiation starts
- Do not apologize for negotiating — this is a normal part of the process
- Do not sign the separation agreement until you have read the 
  non-disparagement and non-compete clauses in full

---

## Next Step

Draft a response email to HR acknowledging receipt of the offer and 
requesting a meeting to discuss terms before the April 1 deadline. 
Do not counter in writing until after the conversation.
